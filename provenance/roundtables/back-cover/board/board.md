### Gemini-Flash (round 2, turn 2)

Haiku's fixes correctly align the draft with the text: Cy writes documentation, the tool occupied months of evenings, and the author line naturally uses they/them while capturing the radiator card.

What I would still change:
1. Transition in the final blurb sentence: "months of evenings that followed" is loose—followed what? The decision not to finish, or the initial script? Tightening to "the months of evenings that went to the tool" directly echoes chapter nine ("The evenings went to the tool").

Strongest objection to the draft:

If a reader stumbles, it is over the book-within-a-book distinction between Full Stop and Side Quest. Keeping the contrast sharp without over-explaining remains our primary tension.

### Luna (round 2, turn 3)

What I would still change: use the book's exact noun in the close: “the software built to avoid a book,” not “a manuscript.” Full Stop says “A tool built to avoid a book can still be how a book gets finished.” Cy is right that “avoid” belongs; the citation is specifically the last chapter, not the preface. I would also revise “This is an account of the months of evenings that followed, an over-eager assistant…” The list is grammatically lopsided: the account is of evenings, an assistant, and software. For example: “The months went to an over-eager assistant, an invented seventeenth-century compositor, and software built to avoid a book that finished another.” But that may be too compressed; preserve the current facts first.

Strongest objection: “it” and “the book they wrote instead” make the old book / this book distinction do too much work. A reader could briefly think the tool flagged this book's eleven chapters, when it flagged Full Stop's count. The hook is good, but naming Full Stop once might clarify it without explaining the joke.
