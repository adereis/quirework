Cy Dequest was writing a book about punctuation. It had eleven chapters, a shelf of disputing sources, and an editor waiting for chapter twelve.

Instead of finishing it, they built a tool to check what the book had promised against what the chapters said. The tool had no opinions about sentences. It kept a list of claims and said so when a chapter disagreed. When they ran it on the book they wrote instead, it flagged the old book's chapter count as an error.

This is an account of a three-year detour into code, an over-eager assistant who invented a seventeenth-century compositor, and the software built to avoid a manuscript that ended up finishing one.

Cy Dequest (pronouns: they/them) is a writer and programmer who lives with notes, scripts, and several shoeboxes of index cards.
