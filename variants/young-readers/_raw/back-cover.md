# Back Cover

Cy Dequest was writing Full Stop, a book about where punctuation marks came from and who spread them. It had eleven chapters, and two of them disagreed with each other. At a small book company, the person whose job was to turn it into a printed book was waiting for chapter twelve.

Instead of finishing it, Cy built a computer program. The program checked what a book had promised against what its chapters said. It had no opinions about sentences. It kept a list of the things the book said were true, and it spoke up when a chapter disagreed. Then Cy wrote a different book and ran the program on that one. The program marked Full Stop's number of chapters as a mistake.

This is the story of the months of evenings that went to the program. It is also about an over-eager computer helper and the printer from the 1600s that the helper made up. And it is about the software built to avoid one book, which finished this one instead.

Cy Dequest's job is writing the instructions that explain a software company's programs. Cy keeps their index cards in shoeboxes and knows where all of them are, except the one that fell behind the radiator.
