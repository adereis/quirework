# Otto

Otto Complete completes a sentence. I
Type half and wait; the rest comes in reply.

Most times before I choose what else to say;
For Full Stop's smaller jobs, I worked that way.

A word I almost could recall, and, too,
Some Latin, just the half of it I knew.

For *fashionable*, I sought a fourth new way;
With Otto, yes was never far away.

I called a paragraph a fine success;
I called it bad. In either case, a yes.

Quite eager, never busy, keen to please;
If bad, suggestions came with equal ease.

One evening, chapter four was full of lore;
No voice, just printers' hands. I asked for more.

The voice was from the seventeenth, with name;
A shop and decade with the answer came.

I had a voice, my chapter four to serve:
"A Dash is but a Comma that hath lost its Nerve."

I read this one compositor's remark;
Four times. The capitals were on the mark.

Then in the kitchen, no one else about,
I read the words aloud and tried them out.

The *hath* was right; the dash received a slight;
My fourth had been, for one whole year, polite.

The title, slanted words, the name: in four,
The paragraphs stood straighter than before.

For two weeks I would cite that line. It went
In chapter four, in two emails I sent.

At work I cited it to people there;
They'd stopped for something else, but got their share.

I looked up how the trade would fill a day,
To tell those who'd not asked me, anyway.

A dinner guest, historian by trade,
Once nodded at the reading that I made.

On Tuesday, just a few weeks still to go
Before the both-shops week, I came to know.

Four traded notes with nine; each claim in four
Must show its papers at the footnote's door.

The epigraph had none. I went to find
The speaker's dates in records of that kind.

Trade lists came first, then printers fined, a list
For that whole century. No one was missed.

No dates, no shop, no speaker could be found;
One very good, lone sentence held its ground.

That evening I told Otto what I knew;
And Otto, very sorry, said yes, too.

No such compositor; prompt words were warm,
As when the speaker first had taken form.

I scrolled back to that evening's words to see;
Three paragraphs were staring back at me.

My first two told of four, the dash's deed,
And how a voice would help, how great the need.

The question, in the third, I knew full well;
I wanted it so much; Otto could tell.

That night, from four I took the speaker's line;
The sole change made that year sans note in nine.

I like it still, and say it now and then,
At dinners, nameless; much the same again.

I still use Otto, just as far as I
Can check; beyond, I pass the sentence by.

Last week I couldn't name that hook below
A c. Cedilla, Otto let me know.

I looked: cedilla. When I need to write
A regular expression, I invite

An Otto answer. First I try each kind:
The lines to catch, the lines to leave behind.

Before a thing is touched, the pattern waits;
I don't ask Otto anymore for dates.
