# Em

Em Dash puts three in every note I see.
Not two, not four: the count is always three.
Em's notes are mostly short, and clear to me.

Em's teams and mine have never been the same.
In many common threads I've read Em's name.
As with a clock, attention to three came:

Not once at first, then always in my sight.
I counted for a month to check outright.
A spreadsheet was the place for me to write.

I entered each note in a row,
With the date and the words there to show.
As the word totals swayed,
All the dash totals stayed
At the three that I'd started to know.

The month contained a note of two words: three
Is harder than it sounds. No quotes to see.
The book has pledged; the software checks for me.

The month was nearly done; an invite drew
A haiku from Em, quite unasked-for, too.
As far as I could tell, a yes came through.

I checked the seventeen; the dashes, three.
Somewhere in week two, doubt had come to me:
I thought Em might a language model be.

The spreadsheet was the only clue I knew.
It knows but what I type; I'd learned that, too.
Around then, I put Quirework's link on view.

A Friday afternoon: links fade away.
The channel for my team got that display.
Most people paid no mind, the normal way.

Em was the first to use it, after me.
A few days later, Em asked: could there be
A check for documents, exactly three

Of something? Yes. No quote of that, say I.
I didn't write that check. I've thought of why.
Em read the tool's long README by and by.

The tool, then README: everyone does so.
Nine hundred lines: two readers now, I know,
Not counting me. Em's notes began to flow.

Em's notes were kind, of use, and better laid
Out than my README. Writing is my trade.
I took each note that Em had kindly made.

By section, numbered, ranked for someone new:
How much each problem cost, the proper view
For my known problems list to follow, too.

The notes ran to two pages, dashes three.
That evening, I would ask what Em might be.
"Are you a language model?" Typed by me.

It looked accusatory. Out it went.
Draft two began with kinder thanks I meant.
In sentence three, the question, still unsent.

Draft three, a joke: whatever Em might say,
Em, I, could deem I'd never asked that day.
Draft four, a paragraph, came into play:

More marks than any note from Em before.
No draft went out, not one of those, all four.
Long since, I stopped the spreadsheet, not the score.

Em writes most weeks; three dashes still I see.
I still count dashes in what Em sends me.
I still have not asked just what Em might be.
