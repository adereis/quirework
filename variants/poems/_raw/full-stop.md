# Full Stop

I didn't choose to stop. No choice was made.
The next two nights, I let its pages lie.
The tool took evenings: months of that same trade.
No one took note. And neither, then, did I.
The wall stayed bare; the paired pinholes stayed too.
Red quoted line six hundred and twelve back.
He'd read the README twice; his question grew.
“Is there a book in here?” was his new tack.
He meant the tool, three years, and me. I said
I didn't know, then wrote this book to see.
The first night, little written, this was read
Just as Full Stop a thousand times for me.
It found one fault, and printed it that night:
The chapter, line, and sentence, all in sight.

“eleven chapters”: this book's count was nine.
The tool I'd built for Full Stop called that wrong.
I looked a while, then wrote a single line
To say to which book that count should belong.
The other book owned that, I plainly wrote.
I ran it once again: it had no more.
For once, no Full Stop grievance in my note.
I'd told it no such thing at all before.
This book was written with it; night by night,
Each chapter faced the promises it made.
It told me when they differed, there in sight.
I fixed most faults its nightly checks displayed.
It told me several times of where they'd split.
I fixed most faults. This book was made with it.

A tool to dodge a book can get one done;
Not that book, though. Full Stop still stays the same.
It has eleven chapters, every one;
Four's shop and nine's still each retain the claim.
I mind no more. The shoeboxes, I know,
Are in a closet. Somewhere boxed in there
The card from back behind the pipes rests, though
I'm still not sure which box. I don't know where.
Last week, four minutes left, she turned her chair
Away from her own screen, as days before.
Then Hedda asked about the book right there.
She'd asked it every time; she asked once more.
My answer was a question, just “Which one?”
She wrote that down, as she had always done.
