Read `variant-common` first: it holds what every edition keeps, and how
an edition is drafted and composed.

The young readers' edition is the same book, chapter for chapter, in the
same three parts, for a reader of about ten who reads well and reads
alone. Each chapter is drafted blind into
`variants/young-readers/_raw/STEM.md`, and composed as
`variants/young-readers/STEM.qmd` with its own sidecar, keeping the
book's file names and titles.

The failures to look for are the ones a children's book invites: a
lesson at the end of a chapter, a word explained in a parenthesis, a
feeling named that the book only shows, a doubt tidied away, a number
made rounder or smaller. Each is a fidelity failure, not a
simplification.

## Blind-draft brief

The reader is about ten, reads well, and reads alone, with no adult
beside them to explain anything. Tell the same story, with the same
people, the same numbers and the same doubts, in the same first person.

Do not condescend. Keep every hard word the story needs and let the
sentence around it carry its meaning; add no glossary, no definition in
brackets, no aside to the reader. Keep the jokes, unexplained. Shorter
sentences are welcome; simpler ideas are not.

Add no moral, no lesson and no reassurance. A chapter ends where the
book's chapter ends, on the same moment, without a closing thought about
what it meant.
