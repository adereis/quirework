The lamp at Gull Point turns once every ten seconds, and visitors ask me why it can't just stay on.
A steady light would be brighter where it points.
It would also look like every other light on the coast.

## A light has to say which light it is

A ship at night sees a dozen lights along the shore.
Most of them are houses, and one of them is me.
The only thing that sets us apart is the pattern: two flashes, a gap, and the long dark.
A captain reads that pattern off a chart and knows where the ship is.[^chart]
`Fl(2) 10s` is how the chart writes it.

## What the turning costs

The lens weighs more than a small car, and it floats on a bath of mercury so one person could push it round by hand.
Nobody pushes it now.
A clockwork drive does, and I wind it every four hours.

*Written at the Gull Point station.
The mistakes are the keeper's own.*

[^chart]: Every light on a chart carries its pattern beside it, in a few letters.
