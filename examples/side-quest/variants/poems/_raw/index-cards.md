# Index Cards

The wall showed chapters; I would see each claim.
I bought white index cards, a box for shoes.
One claim per card: a date, a street, a name.
The front, in just the fewest words I'd use.
The back would show the sentence whence it came,
The chapter and the page, both useful clues.
I copied each by hand till they were done.
*the shop in chapter four made the dash fashionable.* That was one.

Most of a month I wrote; most claims were small.
Eleven chapters held more than I'd thought.
Which shop apprentice wed which daughter: all
Went down by hand. A second box was bought.
I filled the first and labeled both. The wall
Had shown the chapters, not the claims I'd sought.
The first time in a year: I seemed to be
In charge, not with the thing in charge of me.

The cards worked like the wall: at first, all right.
I changed each card to match its sentence, too.
If I could not retrieve one, I would write
A new one; found the old, and now had two.
In my own hand, they disagreed outright.
Each round of four and nine now lumbered through.
Each round took twice as long, a proven cost.
One fell behind the radiator, lost.

I didn't notice. Boxes cannot show
What they are missing. I was unaware.
In March, the heat went off; my arm could go
Behind the pipes. The card for nine was there.
It said what nine still said; none else said so.
I put it back; I am not sure just where.
That chiefly gave a spreadsheet its appeal:
A row per claim seemed like a better deal.

I typed each card, one claim to every row.
I had three hundred rows, and twelve beside.
The chapter, page, and claim, the words that go
With each: four columns that the cards supplied.
I added status, using green to show
A note still matched the sentence by its side.
And yellow stood for check; I marked in red
The notes that contradicted what I'd read.

About a week, the colors stayed all right.
Then orange: “contradicts but I like it,” mostly chapter four.
Four colors, at eleven in the night,
Were more than any head could keep in store.
The Legend tab set meanings out in sight:
A cell per hue, and what each color bore.
I put a note on orange at its head,
Explaining what the orange entry said.

Through most of one full year, I learned to sort
By color, filter chapters into view.
At evening, I could make a full report
Of yellow rows; I had their totals, too.
The sheet knew what I'd typed, and fell far short:
It didn't know what chapters said. I knew
I could not tell if green rows still were green.
The chapters were not what the sheet had seen.

Each time a sentence changed, I had to find
Its row; forgotten, green would simply stay
About a vanished sentence, green and blind.
I write of software; that's what earns my pay.
It took this long to bring that work to mind.
I mention it because of that delay.
One Sunday, I wrote forty lines of code.
Each note, its sentence, formed the script's whole load.

If words had moved or changed, it told me, too.
It had no view of dashes, nothing more.
It didn't know there was a dash to know.
`ch. 8, line 77: changed. The note says 1684; the sentence says 1694.`
I ran it then. It had fourteen to show,
More contradictions than I'd known before.
I thought it broken: one flaw in my book.
For bugs, each item got a closer look.

It wasn't broken. Some concerned the claim
Of those two shops, dressed differently again.
In eight, a date from three was not the same:
A decade's difference stood between them then.
In five, a Scot; the schoolmaster became
Not Scottish when I reached the text of ten.
Three things I'd fixed in rows, each single one,
Were fixes that the book had never done.

For twenty-six whole minutes, I forsook
The practice of the answer in my head.
That Thursday was the first. She asked, “How's the book?”
“I'm building something for chapter four,” instead.
She took a moment longer for that look
Than four allowed. “Good” was the word she said.
Two weeks went by; the document I knew
Was open, with the altered line in view.

She wrote it down: the first change that I knew.
Three years: by seven characters, the line
Was *Book: ch. 4 (tool)*, its wording new.
The script grew; now I needed to define
Which chapters, and what order to go through;
Unsettled notes to leave, by my design.
It grew its own configuration file,
With options I would add at night, a pile.

By morning, I could not account for those.
A second file to say what first was for.
My evenings once were spent on four's old prose;
They went to code, and they improved on four.
Four hadn't said for years what script now shows.
It told me I was wrong, as four before.
Before one second passed, it showed me wrong.
One night, the shops came with the rest along.

I looked up at the chapters, still pinned there,
And at the boxes underneath, to see
What three years' looking finally laid bare:
The sentences had never failed on me.
The claims had only sentences to wear,
With nothing there to bind them, roaming free
Across eleven chapters, scattered wide.
No bond was there to hold them side by side.

That weekend, I took down the chapters there;
The wall had held their pages all before.
That weekend, I left all the plaster bare;
I took the chapters down; they hung no more.
The pins left holes in plaster, pair by pair.
Two weeks: she asked. “I'm building something for chapter four,”
The answer I had given wasn't new.
The answer had not changed. It was still true.
