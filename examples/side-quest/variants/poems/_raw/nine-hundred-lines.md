# Nine Hundred Lines

Red kept on writing “Checking in.”
I answered each, as was my way.
That naming month, what could I say?
The chapter stayed; my words wore thin.

I'd send an outline. Red would see
The book still moved; I'd see where next.
It wasn't just an answer's text:
A plan for chapter twelve, from me.

I opened up a page; up top,
I typed “Chapter twelve” into view.
One was a number; words were two.
I sat with it. That was the stop.

The README filled the window near;
It mostly did. After the name,
Next morning's single page became
The text that now lay open here.

Each evening taught the tool what's new;
Each earned a paragraph; a case
For some meant sections, further space,
To make examples clearer, too.

The settings files had sections there;
For file two, more. Its first intent:
Explain file one. That help was spent;
It no more made the first file clear.

My longest work that year, in view:
A contents list; known faults in place,
Ranked by my shame. A progress face:
The headings gave that semblance, too.

I sent nine hundred lines to Red;
Three lines said work was for the book.
No need to say what place it took;
Instead of it was left unsaid.

I still think this work was my best;
It said just what the tool could do,
And what it said, the tool did, too:
In those three years, above the rest.

The text was clear and all in line;
No part opposed the rest. To say
As much of work I'd sent Red's way
Was more than I could claim for mine.

Documentation is my trade.
And that's the problem. Red read through
The whole, as later I could view
The proof in what his mail relayed.

That day's one line would make me delve;
Months later came another note,
On other things, with this one quote:
He quoted line six hundred twelve.

His answer was one line, no more;
That day, he asked me, plain and clear:
“Is there a chapter in here?”
I read the README through once more.

I read the whole of what I'd charted;
As he would read, to see what lay.
No chapter there. I found that day
A section headed Getting Started.
