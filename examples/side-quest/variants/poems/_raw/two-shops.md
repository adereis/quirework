# Two Shops

My chapter four was on the dash; its name
Was "A Fashionable Interruption." I
Was proud of it; less proud when back it came.

The dash did not spread on its own, said I.
Most marks need type set often, to be read
As normal, not as things that catch the eye.

My shop liked dashes; next door, men instead
Used commas, or no mark. The books it made
Were many, widely read; the dashes spread,

One generation on, throughout the trade.
Of all my work on hands that handled lead,
I think apprentice prose the best I've made.

I had my sources, too, for what I'd said.
The semicolon had its turn in nine;
The quiet, ill-famed mark got care instead.

I gave it sympathy, as when I'd dine
Beside the wedding guest no one had known.
Halfway, I said the dash caused its decline.

The rival's men set dashes of their own,
Across the town, in books that readers knew,
Where others used the quiet mark alone.

One generation on, each shop did too.
Nine followed four in writing by a year.
New sources; four I did not read back through.

I knew what four had said. Red's praise was clear:
He'd read both. Neither shop will get its name.
They've suffered, so have I. No names appear.

The fix looked easy; one deserved the blame,
Or was less right. Which one had got it wrong?
The sources made me shrink four's final claim.

Four was less right; to make its footing strong,
I changed its final third. The claim grew small:
Its shop was early; nine's had brought the throng.

I read four through: no case remained at all.
A smaller claim was simpler to defend;
But who wants merely early? I recall

I gave four back its shop, and tried to mend
The rival's credit, moving doubt to nine.
I typed "helped popularize" to that end.

The phrase meant nothing; still I wrote that line.
And nine had lost its middle to a hole
Where once I'd shown that quiet mark's decline.

That was round one. I won't reveal the whole.
More than I told to Hedda, than I'd say
To you. Each fix gave other notes a role.

Each fix made notes in other chapters stray.
If four claimed credit, nine's notes, by design,
Referred to four; each change made both obey.

Four's note fourteen: "but see note twenty-two in chapter nine,"
Nine's note twenty-two: "for a qualification, see note fourteen in chapter four,"
Now footnotes cited footnotes, line on line.

A reader could keep on, then on some more,
As long as they liked. Both were pinned in view
Above my desk, once printed. Chapter four

Was left, nine right. It helped to see the two,
Like seconds for a duel, on the wall.
Red four, blue nine; where touching, purple hue.

More purple than each other shade in all.
Then came the week both shops could win the day:
A shower thought, where best bad notions call.

Same city, trade and years; why couldn't they,
With clients same, like hands for that last roll
At dinner, reach for dashes their own way?

Each chapter kept its shop; that was the goal.
I took out every note; none need be wrong.
New paragraphs, near twins, now filled the hole.

In both at once, as thoughts may come along,
As both my near-twin paragraphs had said.
The book's best week; I slept well all week long.

When Hedda asked, I all but gladly said
"chapter four". On the seventh day, once more,
I read from top, for joy; the title led.

I read "A Fashionable Interruption." Four
Had called it fashion. That can't happen twice.
The footnotes went back up, just as before.

`notes-old-DO-NOT-DELETE` should now suffice
To tell my faith. I ceased to try to win;
Concealment was my next preferred device.

I tried to keep each blind to its own twin.
I blurred four's date, nine's street, and took a word
Out of four's text. I left the title in.

The word was "fashionable"; I preferred
To leave the title, which I could not bear
To touch. Each small, sound change left sense more blurred.

Both harder reading, no more matched a pair.
I half recalled the comma's chapter two:
A shop, a dash. I found the sentence there.

It gave no credit to the shops I knew:
"the printers of the period," supplied
Safe credit, but a third and different view.

I pinned up two upon four's left-hand side;
Six, on the colon, mentioned that same sign,
So six went up, with two, four, nine beside.

In seven, four's shop better fitted nine;
I pinned it too. Too many seconds, all
No longer facing others in a line.

By spring, I'd no more room upon the wall.
