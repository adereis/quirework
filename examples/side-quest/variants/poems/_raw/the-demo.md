# The Demo

No sentence was my third reply;
She asked, with four to spare.
I said, "Can I show you?" She paused,
Then turned her screen to share.

I sat and ran the tool each night,
With no one there to hear.
My kitchen table was the spot;
The same fault would appear.

I ran the tool on Full Stop there,
Four minutes on my phone.
At first I took nine minutes, while
I spoke to air alone.

By week's end, four would do the job;
I knew what it would show.
I brought the notes I did not need;
I knew where shops would go.

Each night it found the same old fault;
I liked what stayed the same.
The shops in four and nine appeared;
I had a line to name.

I knew the spot upon the screen,
How long the tool would take.
I plugged my laptop in and ran
The tool on their mistake.

It reads the list of claims before
It turns to chapters next.
Each claim gets every sentence shown,
With chapter, line and text.

In less than one brief second came
The shops I'd planned to show.
Most lines were green, too fast to read;
I watched the green ones go.

From four and nine, two lines below
One claim came into sight.
Both said whose shop had spread the dash;
They could not both be right.

The claim was orange, as I'd known
For seven nights before.
Beneath the shops lay three more faults:
I hadn't planned for more.

The night before, I'd made three's date
Agree with chapter eight.
One sentence; then to bed I went,
Pleased at the settled date.

Since that first Sunday, on the list,
The decade held its place.
I hadn't run the tool again
To check the altered case.

I'd practiced showing off the tool,
But not the book I'd changed.
The date lived elsewhere in the book;
Those lines were now estranged.

For years, three other chapters' lines
Had matched with chapter three.
I hadn't told them of the change;
They now could not agree.

On Hedda's screen the date stood once;
Five places lay below.
Three red ones clashed with my new line,
The night before the show.

I had my sentence for the shops;
For this, no words prepared.
I pointed out the claim, then lines,
And named what each declared.

Each chapter, each line's subject named,
Four minutes could not hold
The showing. Hedda read the lines,
Not sums of what they told.

Far easier than all my past
Replies through three long years.
I needn't now describe the fault;
In five lines it appears.

The half hour ended round line three;
She watched the whole display.
Her hard stop was the half hour, yet
She did not turn away.

She pointed at the shops and asked
What orange meant. I knew.
"Contradicts, but I like it,"
She wrote it. Where? No clue.

The screen held four faults at the end;
The count below said four.
She sat back, looking for a while,
Then asked of it once more.

She asked me if the book was done.
"No," was the word I said.
I heard myself. My true reply
Was shorter, now, instead.

For once, the truth took less to tell
Than all my demo's span.
She nodded, wrote, and then we rose,
Nine minutes past the plan.

The notes she shares, I read far more
Than I'd admit. The memo
Had changed again that week. It said:
*Book: demo.*
