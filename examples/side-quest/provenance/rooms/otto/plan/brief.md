# Brief: chapter four of Side Quest, "Otto"

## The book

Side Quest is a short comic memoir by Cy Dequest, a documentation
engineer: Cy writes about software for a living and is not a
programmer. For three years Cy wrote Full Stop, a natural history of
punctuation, which stalled eleven chapters in. Instead of finishing it,
Cy built a tool, Quirework, that checks a book's claims against what the
book has promised. Side Quest tells that detour. It is a preface and
nine chapters in three parts, about twelve thousand words in all:

- Part one, The Book That Disagreed: How's the Book?, Two Shops, Index
  Cards. Written.
- Part two, The Side Quest: Otto, The Name, Nine Hundred Lines.
- Part three, The Book That Got Finished: Em, The Demo, Full Stop.

`_spec.md` is what the book commits to. Read it before anything else.
It outranks this room: a good argument here can show a problem with the
spec, but it does not change what the spec holds. Cy does that.

## The assignment

Chapter four, Otto. It opens part two. About thirteen hundred words, and
that is a ceiling, not a target.

In Cy's words: Otto is the book's argument chapter. An assistant is
useful exactly as far as you can check it, and the compositor is the
proof. Otto is not a villain. Otto is a mirror: "I wanted that sentence
very much, and Otto could tell."

The material, beyond the spec:

- Cy found out on a Tuesday, looking for the compositor's dates for a
  footnote. There were no dates. There was no compositor and no shop,
  only a very good sentence.
- Cy had read the quotation aloud at a dinner, to a historian, who
  nodded.
- Cy still uses Otto, for things Cy can check.

The quotation is given whole, spelled and capitalized exactly as the
spec has it.

## What this chapter leaves alone

- The tool's name. Otto named it, and that is the next chapter, The
  Name. Otto does not name anything here.
- Em Dash, a colleague. Em has the seventh chapter, Em, and is not in
  this one.
- How the book ends. Only the last chapter says that.
- The thesis of part one. Index Cards said, once, that Full Stop's
  claims lived only in sentences. No later chapter says it again.

Where part one leaves off: Cy has written a forty-line script that
checks each note against its sentence, it has found fourteen
contradictions, and it is growing configuration files. It has no name
yet. Cy's manager Hedda's line for the book now reads "Book: ch. 4
(tool)".

## The cast this chapter may use

- **Cy Dequest**, the narrator, in the first person. They/them when
  anyone else refers to Cy.
- **Otto Complete**, the AI assistant Cy used before Quirework existed.
  Eager, agreeable, finishes Cy's sentences. Otto has no pronoun, ever,
  and is never "it". The prose says "Otto" every time, which is a little
  absurd and exactly right.
- **Hedda Count**, Cy's manager (she/her), and **Red Penn**, Cy's editor
  (he/him), only if the chapter needs them.
- The historian at the dinner has no name and no pronoun.

## House rules for the prose

1. No em dashes, anywhere, ever, and not by the back door: three hyphens
   and `&mdash;` both count. Use a colon, a comma, or two sentences.
2. Dry and concrete.
3. Never explain a joke.
4. Short. If a paragraph can go, it goes.
5. US spelling.

The written chapters put one sentence on each line, and a blank line
between paragraphs. The chapter has a heading line, `# Otto`, and
nothing else in the way of headings.
