The narrator spent three years stuck on "chapter four" of Full Stop, their eleven-chapter history of punctuation, because chapters four and nine contradicted each other about which printing shop spread the dash. To find and track such contradictions they built a tool, Quirework, which grew from a wall of pinned pages into index cards, a spreadsheet, a script and then a named piece of software. The tool took over from the book, and when it was shown to the narrator's manager Hedda and to their editor Red, it ended up being how this other, shorter book got written and finished, while Full Stop stayed at eleven chapters.
