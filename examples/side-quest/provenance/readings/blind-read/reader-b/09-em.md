Em is intriguing and the unsent messages feel human, but the repeated three-dash mystery pulls me away from the book-and-tool story just as I expected it to come together.

## What I expect next

Another person using Quirework in a way the narrator didn’t anticipate, and some demonstration of the promises it checks. The new section title makes me expect a finished book, though I don’t yet see how Em’s identity question will lead there.

## What lost me, or what I didn't believe

“The month included a two-word message, which is harder than it sounds.”

Without seeing even a made-up illustration, I can’t tell whether the three dashes are a funny writing habit or an elaborate contrivance. “This book has promised not to, and the software is checking” makes the withholding conspicuous. Also, exactly three dashes in every message sounds more like a deliberate constraint than evidence that someone is a language model. I can believe the narrator becoming obsessed with the pattern; I don’t share his inference.

## More or less?

Less counting, more of Em’s useful response to the tool. The notes ranked by the cost to a newcomer made Em interesting as a person and gave the narrator a needed correction. I liked his reluctance to turn gratitude into an accusation, but wanted more from the first outside user than a punctuation riddle.
